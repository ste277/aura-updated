## 1. Changed wording (one entry)

**Entry 4, Upward line (ūrdhvarekhā), "In this tradition"** (PDF §4.4; commit `ec81a27`, in this PR, not merged):

- **Old (v1.1):** "Later treatises read this line by the finger it reaches. The Hastasañjīvana pairs each finger with a social role of its time: the forefinger with a king or minister, the middle finger with a general, the ring finger with a wealthy merchant or leader, and the little finger with renown. The Sāmudrikatilaka gives a similar scheme, including a king, army commander or preceptor for the middle finger."
- **New:** "Historical Sanskrit palmistry texts describe different interpretations of upward-running palm lines depending on their direction and termination. These interpretations often reflect the social hierarchies, occupations, authority and ideas of prosperity found in the societies in which the texts were composed."

The sources and locators are unchanged: Hastasañjīvana 3.2.80–82; Sāmudrikatilaka 1.152–156; Iyer 70.12; Śārdūlakarṇāvadāna vv. 1–2. The entry's "Context and uncertainty" note is unchanged (it is still subject to W3).

**Please check:** is the new summary historically accurate and adequately supported by these sources? Is "direction" appropriate, or should it read "course"?
